## What to inspect

| Part | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Rotor housings | Chrome flaking or chipping, chatter marks, scoring, seal-track damage; water-jacket corrosion and cracks around ports and dowels; width at Mazda's measuring positions and the variation |
| Side and intermediate housings | Flatness on a reference surface; stepped wear; oil-seal and side-seal tracks; port-edge damage, corrosion, cracks |
| Rotors and seals | Apex- and side-seal groove clearance and carbon packing; corner-seal bores, oil-seal grooves, rotor-bearing bore; heat or carbon damage and cracks between grooves and recesses; seal height, spring free height and free movement |
| Eccentric shaft and bearings | Journal diameter and finish, runout, thrust surfaces; main- and rotor-bearing clearances; end play; oil passages |
| Oil pump (Type A) | Body, tip and side clearances; scoring or embedded debris |

Past its wear or distortion limit, Mazda's instruction is to replace the housing. It gives no lapping allowance. For a borderline iron, my own approach is to measure flatness first, lap on a known-flat plate with figure-eight strokes, turn the housing often so hand pressure can't cause taper, protect the port edges, measure repeatedly, and clean out every trace of abrasive afterwards.
{:.note}

- **Apex-groove clearance:** the manual gives 0.042-0.101 mm in the rotor-inspection procedure and 0.042-0.077 mm in the technical-data table, with 0.15 mm max in both. If you read between 0.077 and 0.101 mm, record both and treat 0.077 as the conservative standard.
- **End play:** the standard is 0.04-0.09 mm with no separate maximum, so change the spacer to bring the measured value into range. Spacers A to E are 7.985, 8.005, 8.025, 8.045 and 8.065 mm. A letter alone doesn't prove the assembled end play. New side seals are chosen by groove rank, not treated like reused ones.

## Packing the rotor seals

1. Fit the inner and outer oil-seal springs in their specified directions.
2. Fit new oil-seal O-rings without twist, then the oil seals over them. Use petroleum jelly only at the manual's retention points and check the seals move freely.
3. Fit the cut-off-seal springs and seals, checking direction, projection and free return.
4. **Front-facing side of the rotor only:** fit each side-seal spring and its matched side seal in its original A/B/C groove, then the corner springs, plugs and corner seals. Line each corner-seal notch up with its apex groove and check the neighbouring side seal still moves.
5. Put the packed rotor on the correct front or intermediate housing, protected face down, engaging the shaft journal without disturbing the oil seals.
6. Fit the long and short apex springs, apex side pieces and main apex seals. The long spring must engage the side-piece stop. Follow Mazda's bonding note for the apex seal and side piece. The side piece must not sit under the next housing.
7. Hold the apex assemblies inward and lower the correctly oriented rotor housing over the rotor. Stop if it needs force.
8. Once the rotor is in its housing, fit the rear-facing side and corner seals and check corner-seal alignment with the SST. Recheck every exposed seal, spring return and apex movement before adding the next iron.
9. Fit new inner and outer water-jacket seals into clean grooves, untwisted, just before closing each joint. Mazda's text on lubricating the water seal is inconsistent (petroleum jelly in one place, no oil or grease in another), so check both notes.

Mazda's caution: only the side and corner seals on the engine-front side go on while the rotor is out of its housing. Photograph each layer and account for every tray position.
{:.note}

## Assembly order and torques

Mazda's order for a Type A engine. Check the shaft turns freely after each layer.

| # | Step | Torque |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Clean front housing on its fixture. Fit the labelled front stationary gear, thrust plate (chamfer towards the gear), needle bearing and plate. | |
| 2 | Lubricate and fit the front rotor, oil-jet ball, spring and plug, then the eccentric shaft. | |
| 3 | Fit the front rotor housing on its dowels with new water seals, controlling all apex pieces. | |
| 4 | Lower the intermediate housing squarely, supporting the shaft as specified. Check dowel engagement. | |
| 5 | Fit the packed rear rotor, rear rotor housing and rear dowel. | |
| 6 | Fit the rear pressure regulator (Type A), rear stationary gear and rear oil seal, meshing the stationary gear with the rotor's internal gear as the rear housing goes on. | |
| 7 | Oil the tension-bolt threads, fit new seal washers, and tighten in the figure sequence over two or three passes. | 31.4-39.2 N·m |
| 8 | Fit the flywheel/counterweight and locknut with the lock and socket. Keep the shaft locked from here on. | 392-490 N·m |
| 9 | Fit the end-float spacer, needle bearing, thrust plate, balance weight, oil-pump parts, OMP drive gear, chain and sprocket. New lock washer on the sprocket nut, then bend it over. | 31.4-46.1 N·m (nut) |
| 10 | Fit the bypass and pressure-regulator valves, springs and plugs to the correct bores. Fit the front cover with its seal. | 18.6-25.5 N·m (cover bolts) |
| 11 | Fit the pulley: oil the lockbolt threads, new O-ring, silicone sealant on the seating face, shaft locked. | 254-294 N·m |
| 12 | Measure end play with everything above fitted (standard 0.04-0.09 mm). Change the spacer only from the measured result. | |
| 13 | Apply the specified sealant bead to the clean oil pan and fit it within five minutes. | 8.8-11.8 N·m (in sequence) |
| 14 | Refit the OMP, labelled lines and nozzles, water pump, thermostat, sensors, hangers, fuel/intake/exhaust parts and ancillaries. Prime every opened oil path rather than relying on dry cranking. | |

More fasteners are on the [torque page](/ProjectPages/RX8/torquespecs/).

## Priming and first start

1. Fill with the running-in oil and fit a new filter.
2. Fill the separate [Sohn reservoir](/ProjectPages/RX8/sohn-adapter/), prime its hose and the OMP inlet, and check the line stays air-free.
3. Fill and bleed the [cooling system](/ProjectPages/RX8/cooling/); check gearbox oil if it was disturbed.
4. Check every fuel joint, earth, starter lead, hose and blanking cap.
5. Disable fuel and ignition positively and crank in short bursts with cooling time. Unplugging the eccentric-shaft sensor does both; Mazda uses it for the compression test.
6. Look for a credible oil-pressure rise and check for leaks between bursts. Re-enable fuel and ignition only once oil pressure is established.

Once it starts, let it warm and stabilise rather than switching off after a few seconds, which can leave a fresh rotary hard to restart. Shut it down at once for no oil pressure, a major leak, a runaway temperature rise or abnormal mechanical noise.
{:.note}

Watch oil pressure, coolant temperature, charging voltage, smoke, leaks and fan operation. Heavy initial smoke from assembly oil and premix should ease. Fine material in the first oil and filter is normal; large flakes, bearing material, coolant contamination or continuing debris are not, so open the first filter and look.

## Running-in and oil changes

The schedule I used on this engine, not a Mazda one:

| Mileage | Oil change |
| ---: | --- |
| 0-100 miles | Initial oil and filter |
| 100-2,000 miles | About every 500 miles |
| Beyond 2,000 miles | About every 3,000-5,000 miles |

Compression started relatively low and rose over roughly the first 1,000 miles. Log raw and 250-rpm-corrected compression (see [compression testing](/ProjectPages/RX8/compression-testing/)), oil and Sohn consumption, coolant level and filter findings at each checkpoint.
